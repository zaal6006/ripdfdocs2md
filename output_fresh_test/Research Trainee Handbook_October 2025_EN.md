## **EQUITY, DIVERSITY AND INCLUSION** 

At The Institute, equity, diversity, and inclusion (EDI) are foundational principles that enhance the quality and impact of our research. By embracing diversity in all its forms, we foster inclusive teams that drive innovation, creativity, and success. We believe our research environment should allow individuals to express their differences—differences that are welcomed, celebrated, and essential to advancing healthcare and science. 

### **OUR COMMITMENT TO EDI** 

##### **Attracting Talent** 

We actively seek to recruit and retain diverse researchers, embedding equity into every stage of the hiring process. 

##### **Building Awareness** 

We empower researchers to integrate EDI principles into their teams and projects through tailored workshops, training sessions, and resources. 

##### **Creating Change** 

Through openness and humility, we listen, learn, and improve. Our research community evolves with a shared commitment to equity and inclusion. 

### **WHY EDI MATTERS** 

##### **Innovative Teams Excel** 

Our diversity encompasses differences in origin, culture, ethnicity, gender or gender identity, language, age, sexual orientation, religion, socioeconomic or hierarchical status, neurodiversity, and visible and non-visible disabilities. Diverse teams are more creative, productive, and flexible, making them better equipped to solve complex challenges. 

##### **A Culture of Belonging** 

We are committed to fostering an environment where every researcher feels respected, valued, and empowered to contribute fully. By recognizing and addressing systemic barriers, we strive to ensure fair access to opportunities for underrepresented groups, including women, Indigenous Peoples (First Nations, Inuit, and Métis), persons with disabilities, ethnic minorities, racialized communities, and members of 2SLGBTQIA+ communities. 

##### **Equity in Opportunity** 

Our goal is to prevent obstacles to attraction, recruitment, retention, recognition, and advancement. Through listening, education, and reflection, we aim to strengthen our research community and create a culture of equity and inclusion that benefits everyone. 

### **ENGAGE WITH EDI** 

The Institute hosts workshops, talks, and panels on topics like inclusive research practices, unconscious bias, and celebrating diversity. Explore the **EDI Event Calendar** to stay updated on upcoming sessions and opportunities. 

**For more information on EDI—including definitions, equity-seeking groups at The Institute, our partners, policy, and action plan—visit our website.**

## **What is a research trainee?** 

A research trainee works under the supervision of a Principal Investigator (PI) at The Institute, within one of its **eight research programs** . Trainees may be appointed at the **undergraduate, MSc, PhD, clinical (MD) fellow, or postdoctoral fellow** level. 

Students must be enrolled full-time in a Canadian university program, while postdoctoral fellows must be registered with McGill University. 

Research trainees typically receive a stipend or bursary from their supervisor’s research grant. This financial support enables you to: 

Conduct your own research or thesis work Contribute to your supervisor’s ongoing research projects Develop valuable skills and experience in a professional research environment 

Research trainees are not considered employees and do not have assigned deliverables or employment contracts. The stipend is meant to support your academic progression and is treated as a **scholarship** for income tax purposes. You will receive a **T4A** and **Relevé 1** tax slip indicating this. 

**Exception:** Postdoctoral fellows are also considered research trainees, but unlike other trainees, federal income tax is deducted directly from their stipend. 

## **Your academic affiliations** 

All research trainees at The Institute are academically affiliated with a **Canadian university** . Your university is responsible for administering your academic program, tuition, and benefits. Depending on your institution, available supports and expectations may vary. 

#### **McGill students** 

If you are registered at McGill University, you are supported by one or more of the following: Graduate and Postdoctoral Studies (GPS) 

- The Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences Your undergraduate program 

#### **Non-McGill students** 

If you are a research trainee from another Canadian university, your home institution remains responsible for your academic registration, program progress, and student services. While you benefit from Institute research resources, we encourage you to access student supports (e.g., international services, wellness, career advising) through your own university. 

#### **International students** 

If you are an international research trainee, you must be registered full-time in a Canadian university program and hold a valid study permit. We strongly recommend beginning the study permit renewal process at least **six months** before the expiry date. 

For support with immigration documents or permits, contact international student services at your home university. If you are a McGill student, visit: <u>www.mcgill.ca/internationalstudents</u>

## **research Responsibilities** 

As a research trainee, you are expected to follow the academic and research policies established **by your university and your graduate program** , which together define the standards for coursework, supervision, and research conduct. If you are registered at **McGill University** , this includes adhering to McGill’s policies on research ethics, academic integrity, and safety. If you are enrolled through another institution, consult your home university’s graduate regulations and research ethics policies. 

It’s important to **stay closely connected with your graduate program staff** —they are your primary point of contact for academic requirements, progress tracking, and support throughout your training. 

The Institute does not maintain separate research integrity policies but does require research trainees to complete all **training relevant to their research activities** . 

#### **McGill University research policies** 

Trainees registered at McGill must follow the university’s official research guidelines, which are outlined in the Research Policy and Guidelines section of the Graduate and Postdoctoral Studies calendar. 

- Regulation on the Conduct of Research 

- Regulations Concerning the Investigation of Research Misconduct 

- Requirements for Research Involving Human Participants 

- Guidelines for Research with Animal Subjects 

- Policy on Intellectual Property 

- Regulations Governing Conflicts of Interest 

- Safety in Field Work 

These policies apply to your research activities whether conducted at McGill, The Institute, or another affiliated site. 

## **Code of Conduct** 

As a research trainee at The Institute, you are expected to uphold the highest standards of professional and personal conduct while carrying out your work. This includes following both: 

- McGill University’s Code of Student Conduct and Disciplinary Procedures (if you are registered at McGill), and 

The Institute’s Code of Conduct, which applies to all members of its research community. 

For research trainees, this means: 

- Treating colleagues, staff, and research participants with respect, professionalism, and civility. Following workplace rules, safety requirements, and mandatory training. Harassment and discrimination are not tolerated under any circumstances. Avoiding conflicts of interest in your research and professional interactions. 

- Maintaining confidentiality, integrity, and responsibility in your research and interactions. 

For full details, please consult The Institute’s Code of Conduct and the McGill Code of <u>Student Conduct and Disciplinary Procedures.</u>

## **Being a Supervisee Working with** Your relationship with your supervisor (PI) is a two-way street. While they guide your research and help **your supervisor** 

Your relationship with your supervisor (PI) is a two-way street. While they guide your research and help you meet program goals, you also play an active role in making the relationship successful. 

#### **Supervision expectations** 

Your supervisor (PI) plays a key role in your academic and professional development. They are responsible for mentoring you in your research and helping you meet the goals of your academic program. At the same time, clear communication, realistic expectations, and a respectful working relationship are essential to your success. 

You can learn more about supervision expectations and best practices at McGill on the Graduate and Postdoctoral Studies (GPS) Supervision page. 

#### **What to do if issues arise** 

A good supervisee: 

Communicates regularly and clearly. Seeks and responds to feedback on time. 

Discusses and agrees on expectations early (e.g., through a Letter of Understanding). Takes initiative in staying on track with research and professional development. Maintains a respectful, professional working relationship. 

If you're experiencing challenges—such as unclear expectations, lack of communication, or concerns about progress—there are several steps you can take: 

Start by speaking with your **supervisor** if you feel comfortable doing so. 

If unresolved, contact your **Graduate Program Advisor** or **Graduate Program Director** . 

For confidential, impartial advice, reach out to the **Ombudsperson for Students** . 

For more detailed guidance on topics like communication, feedback, work-life balance, and career preparation, visit McGill’s Being a Supervisee page. 

For a detailed list of offices responsible for both informal and formal dispute resolution (including filing grievances), please see the guide offered by the Office of the Dean of <u>Students.</u>

## **Vaccination GUIDANCE** 

If your research involves working with **patients, human biological materials, animals, or infectious agents, you may need to meet specific vaccination requirements.** Vaccinations play a key role in protecting your health and the safety of those around you in both clinical and research settings.

## **CLINICAL RESEARCH** 

Clinical research at The Institute plays a pivotal role in advancing healthcare knowledge and patient care. To ensure the safety, integrity, and ethical standards of research, all studies involving human participants, including access to their medical data, must undergo ethics review by a **Research Ethics Board (REB)** recognized within Quebec’s **Réseau de la santé et des services sociaux (RSSS)** . 

For studies conducted solely at The Institute/MUHC, the MUHC Research Ethics Board (REB) is responsible for the review. In the case of multicentre studies, an REB within the RSSS network may act as the reviewing REB. 

Additionally, for any study conducted at or in collaboration with The Institute, **MUHC study authorization** must be obtained from the **_Personne mandatée_ office** before research activities begin. These reviews are essential to protect participants’ rights and well-being while ensuring compliance with national and institutional regulations. 

### **RESEARCH REQUIRING REB REVIEW** 

Research involving human participants (e.g., clinical trials, observational studies) or personal data (e.g., medico-administrative data, medical records) 

Research involving human biological materials, including embryos, fetuses, stem cells, and tissue from both living and deceased individuals 

The Institute follows Quebec's Ministry of Health and Social Services (MSSS) framework, which mandates ethical and legal compliance for all human participant research. 

## **Compliance training for CLINICAL RESEARCH** 

If you are joining a clinical research team, you must complete the required compliance training before taking part in any study activities. The exact courses you’ll need depend on your study’s level—there are seven levels at The Institute, each with specific training requirements based on risk. 

### **REQUIRED TRAINING** 

Depending on the risk level and specifics of your study, you may need to complete one or more of the following training requirements: 

**Human Research SOP Training + Reader:** Must be completed according to the study level. **Good Clinical Practice (GCP):** Essential for all clinical trials. **Health Canada Division 5:** Required for studies involving drugs. **ISO 14155:** GCP-equivalent for medical devices.
